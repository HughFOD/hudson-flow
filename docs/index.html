Do the Hudson River's flow gauges add up?

Water is conserved, so the flow past Green Island should equal the upper Hudson plus the Mohawk, about an hour earlier. This audit checks three U.S. Geological Survey gauges against each other, every 15 minutes from 2022–2025.

Every month of the offset year was above zero. The gap appeared in December 2022 and disappeared in November 2023; outside that year the gauges agree to within 0.7%.

The pattern points to a calibration error. A physical cause would follow the seasons or the flow. A step that switches on and off points to one gauge drifting, then being corrected.

Ruling out the obvious

It isn't the Erie Canal

The canal diverts part of the Mohawk around its gauge, so it was the first suspect. But the gap is the same with the canal open (591 cfs) as closed (639 cfs), and it moved the expected way at only 3 of 5 openings and closings.

Method

How it works

From raw gauge readings to an audited balance and a forecast.

  1. Collect

    15-minute readings from three USGS gauges and hourly basin rainfall from Open-Meteo.

  2. Clean

    Flag stuck sensors, spikes, ice and values USGS marks as estimated; 4.7% of readings are left out.

  3. Balance

    Compare Green Island with the upstream total, with travel times between gauges fitted from the data.

  4. Forecast

    Predict Green Island 12 hours ahead with boosted trees, tested walk-forward with calibrated ranges.

What the numbers mean

The terms used on this page.

cfs
Cubic feet per second: the volume of water passing a gauge each second. One cubic foot is about 28 litres.
Gap
Flow at Green Island minus the two upstream gauges combined. Zero means the gauges agree.
No change
The baseline forecast: assume the flow 12 hours from now equals the flow now.
90% range
The span the model expects the actual flow to fall inside 90% of the time.
